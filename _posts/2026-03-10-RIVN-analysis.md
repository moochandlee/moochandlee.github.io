## Executive Summary

Since its founding in 2009, Rivian’s performance has been defined by persistent efforts to navigate the fiercely competitive automotive sector, characterized by high large capital expenditures and complex supply chains. 

Rivian was one of the first to carve out a niche in the North American electric truck/SUV segment, which in 2011 at the time of their pivot was non-existent.

The company follows a similar sales and delivery model as Tesla, taking preorders before production begins through their website, and fulfilling deliveries directly to customers’ home addresses or to service centers across the country, with no traditional dealership experience. 
Rivian differentiates itself further against legacy automakers and more established EV manufacturers with heavy investment into their EV technology for multifaceted use within both consumer and commercial markets. This strategic advantage stems from a flexible manufacturing platform that is complemented by the commitment to a high-volume commercial fleet for Amazon as well as proprietary software licensed to VW Group. 

Despite efforts to achieve profitability, cost reduction for their current products remains meager and high costs for a manufacturing facility meant for the R2 and R3 have delayed the timeline to profitability indefinitely.
