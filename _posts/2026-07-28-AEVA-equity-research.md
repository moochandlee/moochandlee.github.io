## Executive Summary

Aeva Technologies possesses what we believe is the most technologically differentiated LiDAR sensor on the market. Its Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) 4D LiDAR-on-chip measures instantaneous velocity per pixel — a capability that no Time-of-Flight (ToF) competitor can replicate — and has earned reference-sensor status on NVIDIA's DRIVE Hyperion platform and a coveted Tier-1 supplier position with a top European passenger OEM (widely believed to be Mercedes-Benz). Daimler Truck has begun receiving Atlas sensors, and non-automotive verticals (defense, industrial, smart infrastructure) are diversifying the revenue base.

However: Aeva's most significant revenue opportunities are gated behind 2028 start-of-production timelines. The company will generate only ~$33M in revenue in FY2026 while burning ~$100M in cash. The stock, even after a ~53% decline from its $31.30 high, still trades at ~32x forward sales — a premium to Ouster (~11x) and Hesai (~5x) that already prices in successful execution of programs that are two years away.

We initiate at **Equal-Weight** with a $20 price target, representing ~36% upside. Aeva is the right horse in the right race, but the timeline is long, the cash burn is real, and the current valuation leaves limited margin for error. We would become more constructive on a pullback to $10-12 or on tangible evidence of pull-forward in the automotive revenue timeline.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2017 by former Apple engineers Mina Rezk and Soroush Salehian Dardashti, Aeva Technologies develops 4D LiDAR sensors based on Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) technology integrated onto a silicon photonics chip.

### Technology: FMCW vs. ToF

| Attribute | Aeva FMCW 4D LiDAR | Competitor ToF LiDAR |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------------------|
| **Velocity measurement** | Instantaneous per pixel | Requires multi-frame tracking |
| **Interference immunity** | Inherent (coherent detection) | Susceptible to crosstalk |
| **Range** | Up to 500m (Atlas Ultra) | 250-300m typical |
| **Silicon photonics** | Yes (single chip, scalable) | No (discrete components) |
| **Cost at scale** | Lower (CMOS-compatible mfg) | Higher (more components) |
| **Production readiness** | In validation (2026) | Proven at scale (Hesai: 100K+ units/yr) |

The FMCW advantage is real and meaningful: by measuring velocity directly (via Doppler shift), Aeva's sensors can instantly distinguish a stationary object from a moving one, detect the speed and direction of pedestrians and vehicles in a single frame, and operate without interference from other LiDAR sensors — a critical feature as autonomous vehicle density increases.

## Industry & Competitive Analysis

### The LiDAR Market in 2026

The Western LiDAR market is undergoing a brutal consolidation. Luminar — once the sector's flagship — is approaching insolvency after losing Volvo as its anchor customer. Innoviz is surviving on NRE payments with minimal sensor sales. MicroVision and Cepton have effectively exited. This leaves three investable Western LiDAR companies:

| Company | Technology | FY2026E Revenue | Cash Position | Path to Profitability |
|---------|------------|----------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Ouster (OUST)** | Digital ToF + cameras + edge AI | ~$223M | $175M, zero debt | 2027 (targeted) |
| **Aeva (AEVA)** | FMCW 4D LiDAR | ~$33M | ~$225M (incl. facility) | 2029+ |
| **Innoviz (INVZ)** | MEMS ToF | ~$83M (mostly NREs) | Lower burn (NRE-funded) | 2027+ (uncertain) |
| **Hesai (HSIA)** | ToF (various) | ~$650M | Strong; already profitable | Already profitable |

Ouster has repositioned itself as a "Physical AI sensing platform" — combining LiDAR, cameras, edge AI compute, and software (via the StereoLabs acquisition). Its Q1 2026 product revenue alone ($49M) exceeded Aeva's entire FY2026 guidance. It's the safer, more diversified LiDAR investment.

Hesai is the elephant in the room. The Chinese LiDAR leader is already profitable, manufactures at massive scale (100K+ units/year), and competes directly with Aeva at Mercedes and other OEMs. Its cost advantage is structural and unlikely to be closed by any Western LiDAR company.

**Aeva's niche:** FMCW's velocity-sensing and interference immunity provide genuine differentiation for OEMs pursuing Level 3/4 autonomy. If the technology thesis plays out — if OEMs converge on FMCW as the standard for high-end ADAS — Aeva wins. But this convergence is not guaranteed, and ToF sensors continue to improve on the metrics where they trail (velocity estimation via software, interference mitigation via signal processing).

### Why Not Higher?

1. **The European OEM program is the entire thesis.** At a 25% probability of "full execution" — which requires the OEM program to reach volume production on time, with multiple models, across global markets — a ~$20 fair value already embeds optimism.
2. **Time value of money works against early-stage LiDAR companies.** Three years of discounting at a 12% WACC erodes the present value of 2028+ cash flows by ~29%.
3. **Hesai's profitability and Ouster's diversification provide safer ways to play LiDAR.** Aeva must be right about both technology (FMCW wins) and execution (manufacturing at scale) — a parlay that warrants a discount.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Aeva's FMCW technology is adopted as the industry standard for L3+ autonomous driving. The European passenger OEM program (Mercedes) ramps on schedule in 2028 across multiple vehicle models globally. Daimler Truck volumes scale to 50K+ units annually. A third major OEM signs, validating the platform. Revenue reaches $500M+ by 2029 with 30%+ gross margins. The silicon photonics manufacturing approach proves its cost advantage as volumes scale. In this scenario, Aeva trades to 15x 2030E revenue, implying a $4.5B+ market cap and a stock price of $60+.

### Bear Case

Automotive OEMs delay or cancel L3 autonomy programs as regulatory hurdles and liability concerns mount. The European OEM program is pushed to 2029 or cancelled. ToF sensors improve their velocity estimation, eroding FMCW's differentiation. Hesai undercuts on price at the same OEMs. Aeva runs out of cash in 2027 and is forced to raise equity at distressed levels, or is acquired for its IP at a modest premium to cash. Stock: $4-6.

### Our View

We are constructive on the technology and the team but cautious on the valuation and timeline. The key question for investors is: **how much of the 2028+ upside is already in the stock at $14.70?** We believe the answer is "the majority of it." At ~32x forward sales, Aeva trades at a significant premium to Ouster (~11x) despite having 85% less revenue and a cash-burn rate that will require additional capital before its anchor program reaches production. The stock is a call option on FMCW becoming the industry standard — a thesis we believe in, but one that doesn't justify paying 2028 multiples in 2026.

**We would become buyers on a pullback below $10 or on concrete evidence that the automotive revenue timeline is being pulled forward** — for example, an additional OEM win with an earlier SOP date, or faster-than-expected Daimler Truck ramp.

## Risk Factors

1. **SOP timeline risk.** The European OEM program's 2028 start-of-production date is not guaranteed. Automotive development cycles routinely slip. A one-year delay would burn an additional $100M+ in cash and push the revenue inflection to 2029+.

2. **Concentration risk.** Aeva's equity story depends on ~2-3 programs (European OEM, Daimler Truck, Top-5 OEM). Loss of any one would be catastrophic to the investment thesis.

3. **Cash burn and dilution.** Even with the June 2026 follow-on, Aeva will need to fund 2+ years of operations before the European OEM program generates meaningful revenue. The Sylebra preferred facility provides a backstop, but using it would be dilutive to common equity.

4. **Technology risk.** FMCW LiDAR has not been manufactured at automotive scale. Silicon photonics integration is proven in data centers, not in vibration-prone, temperature-extreme automotive environments. Yield issues at scale could compress margins below expectations.

5. **Competitive obsolescence.** ToF LiDAR is not standing still. Hesai, Ouster, and others are improving range, resolution, and interference rejection with each generation. If the performance gap narrows sufficiently, OEMs will choose the cheaper, proven technology.

6. **Chinese competition.** Hesai is already profitable and manufactures at 100K+ units annually. If Western OEMs become comfortable with Chinese LiDAR suppliers (a geopolitical "if"), Aeva's cost disadvantage becomes structural.

7. **Insider selling.** Management and insiders sold ~$20M in stock over the 3 months through mid-July 2026. While insider selling can be motivated by personal financial planning, the magnitude and consistency warrant monitoring.
