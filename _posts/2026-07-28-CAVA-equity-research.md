## Executive Summary

Cava Group is the category-defining brand in Mediterranean fast-casual dining — a segment it effectively created and now dominates. With 459 locations generating $3.0M average unit volumes at 25.1% restaurant-level margins (ahead of Chipotle's 23.7%), a 9.7% same-store sales growth rate driven entirely by traffic (+6.8%), and a 1,000-location long-term target against 459 today, Cava offers a rare combination of unit growth and comp momentum in a consumer environment where most peers are struggling to generate either.

The stock has pulled back ~34% from its 52-week high of $98.79 on broader consumer spending concerns and an elevated forward P/E of ~150x. However, we believe the pullback creates an attractive entry point: Cava's Q1 2026 beat-and-raise quarter (+32% revenue growth, raised full-year comp guidance) demonstrates that the business is accelerating, not decelerating. At $65, the stock trades at ~33x FY2027E EV/EBITDA — expensive on a static basis but reasonable for a compounder with a 15%+ unit growth and high-single-digit comp algorithm that we expect to persist for the next 5+ years.

We initiate with an **Overweight** rating and a $98 price target, derived from a 10-year DCF (10% WACC, 3% terminal growth) cross-checked with 35x FY2027E EBITDA — in line with high-growth restaurant compounders at similar stages (Chipotle 2014, Wingstop 2018, Domino's 2012).

## Company Overview

Founded in 2006 and going public via IPO in June 2023, Cava Group operates and franchises fast-casual Mediterranean restaurants under the Cava brand. The concept is built around customizable bowls and pitas with a Mediterranean flavor profile — grilled proteins, seasonal vegetables, house-made dips and spreads, and grain bases — served in a Chipotle-style assembly line format.

**Key business model characteristics:**

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Concept** | Fast-casual Mediterranean bowls & pitas |
| **Store count** | 459 locations (Q1 2026), 29 states + DC |
| **Ownership** | ~95% company-operated, ~5% franchised |
| **AUV** | $3.0 million (flat YoY, lapping strong 2024 comps) |
| **Restaurant-level margin** | 25.1% (Q1 2026) |
| **Digital mix** | 37.9% of revenue (approaching 40%) |
| **Unit growth target** | 75-77 net new openings in FY2026 (~17% growth) |
| **Long-term target** | 1,000 locations by 2032 |
| **Vertical integration** | In-house production of dips/spreads (harissa, tzatziki, hummus); CPG products in 650+ Whole Foods locations |
| **Balance sheet** | Net cash positive; debt-to-equity 0.6x; current ratio 2.7x |

Cava's vertical integration is an underappreciated moat. The company manufactures its signature dips and spreads in-house, ensuring consistency across locations while creating a secondary revenue stream through CPG distribution in 650+ grocery stores. This dual model (restaurants + CPG) mirrors early Chipotle and acts as both a margin lever and a brand awareness channel — a customer who tries Cava harissa at Whole Foods is more likely to visit a restaurant.

## Industry & Competitive Analysis

### The Fast-Casual Landscape in 2026

The fast-casual restaurant industry is under pressure. Inflation fatigue has pushed consumers toward value, and the segment broadly lost share of wallet through Q3 2025. However, the pain is unevenly distributed:

- **Chicken concepts (Chick-fil-A, Raising Cane's):** Gaining share. Strong value perception.
- **Coffee (Starbucks, Dutch Bros):** Resilient. Habitual purchase, small ticket.
- **Mediterranean (Cava):** Growing. Health perception + flavor differentiation.
- **Premium salad (Sweetgreen):** In distress. -12.8% comps in Q1 2026. Consumers reject $20 salads.
- **Mexican (Chipotle):** Flat comps. Scale advantage but concept fatigue. Traffic declining.
- **Better burger (Shake Shack):** Mixed. Premium fatigue but strong brand.

### Competitive Positioning

Cava's key competitive advantages:

1. **Category leadership with no scaled direct competitor.** There is no national Mediterranean fast-casual chain at Cava's scale. Regional players (Naya, Roti, Mezeh) are 10-50 locations vs. Cava's 459. This first-mover advantage in a growing cuisine category is structurally similar to Chipotle's position in Mexican fast-casual in 2005-2010.

2. **Health perception tailwind.** Mediterranean cuisine benefits from a "health halo" — olive oil, lean proteins, vegetables, whole grains — that Mexican fast-casual (cheese, sour cream, tortillas) does not. As GLP-1 drugs and health consciousness reshape consumer preferences, Mediterranean concepts are net beneficiaries.

3. **Better unit economics than peers.** At 25.1% restaurant-level margins, Cava is more profitable per store than Chipotle (23.7%) and dramatically ahead of Sweetgreen (~10%). This is driven by a simplified kitchen (no fryers, no grills — just a flattop and prep stations), high throughput (assembly line format), and in-house dip production.

4. **AI citation dominance.** AI platforms (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) now default to recommending Cava for "best Mediterranean chain" and "best healthy fast food." This is a structural competitive advantage — Cava captures organic search traffic that competitors must pay for.

### Growth Algorithm

Cava's growth is driven by two engines:

**Unit growth (15-17%):**
- 75-77 net new openings guided for FY2026 (~17% unit growth).
- New units consistently open above 100% productivity (AUV > $3.0M).
- ~65% of new units are in existing markets (density play), ~35% in new markets.
- 1,000-location target by 2032 implies a 14% unit CAGR from current levels.

**Same-store sales (4.5-6.5% guided for FY2026, raised from 3-5%):**
- Q1 2026 comp of +9.7% (traffic +6.8%, price/mix +2.9%).
- Took only 1.4% pricing in early 2026; no additional pricing planned.
- Menu innovation (salmon, roasted white sweet potato) driving incrementality.
- Digital mix approaching 40% supports higher check sizes.

**Combined: ~20-22% annual revenue growth at current algorithm.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Cava executes its growth algorithm with precision — 75+ openings per year, mid-to-high single-digit comps, and margin expansion through manufacturing scale and G&A leverage. The Mediterranean category continues to gain share of US restaurant spend as health-conscious consumers shift away from heavier formats. The company reaches 800+ locations by 2029 with sustained 25%+ restaurant-level margins, at which point the compounding math supports a 10-year DCF value of $135+. The stock re-rates as the market begins pricing it as a compounder rather than a concept stock.

### Bear Case

Fast-casual broadly loses relevance as GLP-1 adoption reduces overall food-away-from-home demand. Cava's comps decelerate to flat as the concept matures faster than expected in core markets. New market entries (Southeast, Texas, Midwest) underperform relative to coastal strongholds. Input cost inflation (lamb, olive oil, labor) compresses margins despite pricing. Growth slows to 50 openings/year and the stock de-rates to sector-average multiples — implying $42/share, or ~35% downside.

### Our View

We see the bull case as more likely than the bear case for one simple reason: **Cava is still in the early innings of unit growth**. At 459 locations, it has 2.3x less penetration than Chipotle (4,000+), and the Mediterranean category is underpenetrated relative to Mexican fast-casual. The key risk — comp deceleration — is actively being disproven by the data (Q1 2026 comps of +9.7% were the strongest in the sector). We believe the recent stock pullback from $98 to $65 reflects broader consumer sector rotation, not company-specific deterioration, creating a buying opportunity.

## Risk Factors

1. **Valuation compression risk.** At ~33x FY2027E EBITDA, any growth disappointment triggers outsized multiple contraction. If comps slip to 2-3% or openings miss by 10%+, the stock could re-rate to 20x or below.

2. **Concept maturation.** Every restaurant concept eventually saturates. Cava's core markets (DC, NYC, LA, Boston) are approaching density. New market entry (Texas, Southeast) carries higher risk and lower initial AUVs.

3. **Input cost exposure.** Mediterranean cuisine is exposed to specific commodities — olive oil, lamb, chickpeas, feta — that have volatile global supply chains and limited hedging options compared to beef or chicken.

4. **Labor cost pressure.** Restaurant industry wage inflation continues at 4-5% annually. Cava has offset this with throughput improvements, but sustained wage pressure could compress margins if productivity gains plateau.

5. **Consumer spending sensitivity.** Fast-casual is a discretionary purchase. In a recession, consumers trade down to QSR or cook at home. Cava's $13 average check positions it better than Sweetgreen ($18) but worse than McDonald's ($8).

6. **GLP-1 risk (overblown but real).** GLP-1 weight-loss drugs reduce overall food consumption. Early data suggests fast food and snacks are most exposed; fresh/healthy concepts may actually benefit. But the magnitude and persistence of this effect remain unknown.
